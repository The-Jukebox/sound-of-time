EARTH ARCHIVE — RECORD 003: THE FOREST

NASA GEDI L4B/L3 & MODIS NDVI
DATASET: Gridded GEDI Vegetation Structure Metrics & Biomass Density (L4B & L3)
NASA SOURCE: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center / LP DAAC (DOI: 10.5067/GEDI/GEDI04_B.002)
INSTRUMENT: Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation (GEDI) 1064nm Lidar on the ISS
VARIABLES: Canopy Height (rh100, m), Canopy Cover, PAI (m²/m²), FHD Diversity, Biomass (Mg/ha)
TIME PERIOD: 2019-04-18 to 2023-03-16 (Calibrated orbital laser reanalysis; not a live sensor link)
GEOGRAPHIC AREA: Global Forest Transects: Hoh Rainforest, Amazon Basin, Smoky Mtns, Black Forest, Redwoods

HOW DOES THE FOREST BECOME MUSIC?

  • Real Numerical Data: Every musical parameter is mathematically generated from authentic NASA GEDI satellite laser altimeter waveforms measuring 3D canopy profiles.
  • Canopy Height (rh100) → Musical Pitch: Height of the tree crowns (8m to 68m) maps to notes across a multi-octave Earth Lydian scale. Massive old-growth redwoods sing in bright, ringing bell tones; pioneer saplings resonate in grounding mid-register notes.
  • Canopy Cover → Harmonic Richness & Filter Cutoff: Denser canopy closure (0.85 to 0.95) opens resonant lowpass filter cutoffs (600Hz to 2800Hz) and introduces warm spatial chorus overtones.
  • Foliage Height Diversity (FHD) → Melodic Complexity: Vertical multi-tier structural complexity controls the density of secondary arpeggios, generating delicate counterpoint patterns.
  • Aboveground Biomass (AGBD) → Sub-Bass Foundation: High carbon biomass density (>350 Mg/ha) triggers deep fundamental sub-bass resonance anchoring the forest floor.
  • Scientific & Acoustic Disclosure: Scientific laser data mathematically drives the musical composition; natural acoustic layers (wind, leaves, birds, stream, cicadas) are procedurally modeled and synthesized inspired by National Park Service Natural Sounds ecological surveys.

DEPTH: Z = -5.0 meters (Surface Ocean Layer)

HOW WAS THIS SOUND MADE?

  • Real Numerical Data: Sound is mathematically driven by real daily mean measurements from NASA's ECCO state estimate (combining satellite altimetry, Argo floats, and MITgcm physics).
  • Temperature → Pitch: Ocean temperatures (4°C to 28°C) map to notes across an oceanic Dorian scale. Subpolar waters sing in resonant low bass; warm Gulf Stream waters sing in high melodic pitches.
  • Thermal Delta → Duration & Melody: Sharp thermal fronts produce faster note cadences and harmonic shimmer; stable waters produce long, calming ambient pads.
  • Salinity → Timbre: Higher salinity (36.5+ PSU) expands FM modulation depth and opens lowpass filter cutoff frequencies, creating a brighter, crystalline texture.
  • Current Speed → Rhythm & Stereo Panning: Flow velocity controls stereo panning oscillations and dynamic swell intensity.
  • Scientific Disclosure: This generated music is a structured data sonification, not an underwater microphone (hydrophone) recording.

HOW DOES THE ATMOSPHERE BECOME SOUND?

  • Real Numerical Data: Sound is driven by real NASA MERRA-2 and GPM satellite observations of global storm fronts and air pressure.
  • Precipitation Rate → Rain Density: Total surface rain flux (0.0 to 30.0 mm/hr) dynamically controls procedural water droplet impact velocity, density, and throughfall timbre.
  • Surface Wind Speed → Wind Filter & Pitch: 10-meter wind velocity (0 to 35 m/s) modulates lowpass filter cutoffs (300Hz to 1800Hz) and generates aerodynamic shear whistle overtones.
  • Barometric Pressure → Fundamental Harmonics: Surface pressure (970 to 1035 hPa) maps to notes across the atmospheric Dorian scale. High pressure anticyclones resonate in crystalline highs; deep gale lows sing in ominous sub-bass.
  • Lightning & Convective Instability → Thunder: High convective lightning flash density (>12 flashes/km²/yr) triggers rolling sub-bass thunder discharges and stereo reverberation.
